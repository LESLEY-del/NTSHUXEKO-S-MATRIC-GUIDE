Subject guide

Pure Mathematics

Mathematics improves when you solve questions yourself. Reading worked examples helps, but you also need to practise without looking at the solution.

  1. Review important Grade 11 skills that are used again in Grade 12.
  2. Write down the method and every calculation step; do not skip working.
  3. After a mistake, identify exactly where your method went wrong and redo the question.
  4. Practise both Paper 1 and Paper 2 throughout the term.

Topics to give attention to:

AlgebraNumber patternsFunctionsProbabilityFinancial mathematicsCalculusGeometryTrigonometry
Try this: Set a timer for 25 minutes, complete a small set of questions, then spend 10 minutes marking and correcting them.

Subject guide

Physical Sciences

Physical Sciences combines concepts, definitions, equations and calculations. Learn what each quantity means before trying to memorise formulas.

Paper 1 — Physics:

  • Mechanics and motion
  • Waves, sound and light
  • Electricity and magnetism / electric circuits
  • Definitions, principles and multiple-choice practice

Paper 2 — Chemistry:

  • Organic chemistry
  • Electrochemistry
  • Acids and bases
  • Chemical calculations, definitions and multiple-choice practice
Calculation tip: Write the formula, substitute the known values with units, calculate carefully and check whether your answer makes sense.

Revision approach

Geography & Life Sciences

Learn the meaning of each concept before memorising details. Diagrams, labelled processes, maps and short summaries can make revision easier.

  • Explain a process in your own words.
  • Practise interpreting diagrams, graphs, maps and data.
  • Use past papers to learn how questions are phrased.
  • Mark your work and revise the concepts you missed.
✍️
Reading & writing

Home Languages

Regular reading can improve comprehension, vocabulary and writing. Practise the different question types required by your language paper.

  • Read a passage and summarise its main idea.
  • Practise comprehension questions and support answers with evidence from the text.
  • Plan essays before writing and leave time to proofread.
  • Revise the formats and language rules taught in class.
🗓️
Build a routine

A simple weekly study plan

Choose a realistic timetable that fits school, homework, rest and other responsibilities. Short, focused sessions repeated regularly are useful.

1. Learn

Review class notes and make sure you understand the main concept.

2. Practise

Answer textbook exercises or past-paper questions without copying.

3. Review

Mark your answers, correct mistakes and revisit weak areas later.

🎯
Don't overlook it

Life Orientation

Take Life Orientation seriously and keep track of tasks, projects and examination preparation. Read each instruction carefully and make sure you answer what is being asked.

  • Record due dates in a calendar.
  • Use the rubric or instructions provided by your teacher.
  • Prepare for assessments in advance instead of leaving everything until the last day.
📝
A method you can reuse

How to use past question papers

  1. Choose a paper that matches the subject and paper you are revising.
  2. Attempt it under timed conditions when you are ready.
  3. Mark it with the correct memorandum where available.
  4. Keep a list of mistakes and the topics you need to revisit.
  5. Redo difficult questions a few days later to check that you understand them.
Remember: A past paper is a practice tool, not a prediction of the next examination. Use it alongside your class notes, textbooks and teacher's guidance.
